The data layer

What a field-level dictionary has to carry, and when to fill it in.

Companion to The Honest Mockument · Version 1.0 · 18 August 2026

1. The problem this solves

Field-level work — where each number comes from, how it is calculated, what it is worth checking against — is the most expensive part of specifying an app, and the easiest to do at the wrong time.

Do it too late and the screens are already agreed when somebody discovers that the central figure is only produced monthly, or does not exist at all. Do it all up front and most of the effort is spent on fields belonging to pages that get cut, redesigned or postponed. In a typical dictionary the majority of fields are calculated rather than copied, so "all up front" is not a small bill.

The way out is to notice that data questions divide cleanly in two.

Questions that can invalidate a page. Does this number exist at all? Who supplies it? What point in time is it as of, and how long after that can a person see it? Is it a stored fact or something we compute? These are design questions wearing a data costume: a figure that only closes monthly kills a daily tile no matter how good the layout is. They take seconds to answer, only the business can answer them, and they belong in the mockument from the first day.

Questions that can only change the code. Which system, which field, the exact formula, the display format, the routing when the same figure arrives from two places, the reconciled value for a known example. None of these can invalidate a screen. They are also most of the work.

2. The ladder

Field detail is therefore not a phase. It is a ladder, climbed per page, so nothing above the bottom rung is ever paid for on a page that does not survive review.

Rung What is answered When Fields
L0 · exists What it means in business words · one value or a list · what it is as of · when a person can see it · who supplies it With the mockument. Required to pass the business-review gate. means holds periodicity visibleWhen provenance
L1 · display Unit or format · what an empty value shows · what a stale value shows After the page passes business review unit nullRule staleRule
L2 · sourced System · feed, table or field · calculated or not · the calculation · what it derives from · sensitivity Before the page is built. Required to pass the implementation gate. source calculated calculation derivedFrom sensitivity
L3 · verified Evidence the lineage was traced · the tolerance a test may use At QA, before release evidence tolerance

Hero fields are the exception. Some numbers are the product — a score, a ranking, a figure somebody would have to defend under scrutiny. Validating pages that display a wrong number proves nothing, so a hero field goes to L2 while the mockument is still being drawn. Expect a handful, not fifty.

Promotion is per page. The same field may sit at L2 because one page is being built, and be irrelevant everywhere else. Nothing forces a whole dictionary to move at once.

A field is defined once and referenced everywhere. Give each field a stable identifier. A figure that appears on nine screens then has one meaning, one source and one calculation, and where two pages describe it differently the register shows the disagreement rather than averaging it away.

3. What a dictionary row should carry

Split into what the catalogue holds once per field, and what each page's use of it records.

Per field, once:

Column Why it is there
Identifier So nine appearances share one definition
Business meaning In the words somebody would use to a customer, not the column name
Holds One value, or a list
Unit and format Including scale — the ×1,000 and ÷100 errors are the two most common production data defects, and prose is where they hide
Null rule What appears when there is no value
Stale rule What appears when the value is older than it should be. Different question, different answer, and the one that actually happens
Periodicity Which fixed value describes what it is as of
Visibility How long after that point a person can see it, stated as a business interval, never as a job name
Provenance Held by us · a provider · the user · computed · nobody knows
Source The system, feed or table
Calculated Yes / no — and if yes, the calculation
Derives from The identifiers of the fields it is computed from. Makes "which screens break if this source is late?" a query rather than an investigation
Sensitivity Personal, restricted, public — it drives test data and access, and somebody will ask for the list
Tolerance What counts as a match when checking. Without it, "does it agree?" is untestable for anything with decimals
Evidence Where the lineage was traced, and when

Per appearance, on the page:

Column Why it is there
Element identifier Which thing on which page shows this field. This is the join that makes the dictionary and the mockument one system
Sample value The value drawn in the mock, so the sketch, the screenshot and the build agree
Rung How far this field has been taken for this page

4. Vocabularies

Three columns must use fixed vocabularies. Free text in them cannot be counted, filtered, tested or trusted, and drifts into dozens of near-synonyms within months.

Periodicity — live · end of previous business day · end of previous month · latest closed period · on demand · reference / on change. A project may extend the list with a period its own work actually uses — a trading day, a filing, a shift, a billing cycle — but it extends the list deliberately, in one place.

Visibility — a business interval: "next business day", "second to fourth working day of the month", "immediately on save". Never a job or script name.

Provenance — held by us · a provider · the user · computed · nobody knows.

Nuance goes in an adjacent note, never in the vocabulary column itself.

Two things that are commonly filed as periodicities and are not. A derived rate — a figure expressed per year, per head, per unit — is a calculation, computed as of a point that is itself one of the values above. And a refresh cadence is not a periodicity either: a weekly job that reloads figures published quarterly does not make the data weekly.

5. Ten defects worth checking for

These recur in almost every field-level dictionary. Each is cheap to fix and expensive to leave.

  1. Lineage that covers a fraction of the fields while the introduction implies it covers all of them. Either finish it, or narrow the stated scope to the fields whose source genuinely varies.
  2. No reconciled examples. Two or three known records, traced end to end with their expected values, are what turn a dictionary from documentation into a test.
  3. Vocabulary drift — six defined values, seventeen in use. Bind the columns to dropdowns and move the nuance into notes.
  4. Open flags with no owner and no date. A question mark in a notes column is not a question; it is a shrug. Split it into question · who must answer · needed by · ticket.
  5. Transformations described in prose. "Divided by a hundred", "in millions". Give scale and sign their own columns.
  6. No dependency edges, so nobody can answer "if this source is late, which screens go blank?"
  7. No tolerance, so equality checks on anything with decimals are undefined.
  8. Sensitivity buried inside a free-text column rather than being a field you can filter on.
  9. No stale rule, in a system whose data is regularly late by design.
  10. No link back to the screen specification, so the dictionary and the mockument drift into two documents describing two different products.

6. Joining the dictionary to the mockument

One identifier in each direction, and the two artefacts become one system:

element in the mockument                  dictionary row
────────────────────────                  ──────────────
EL-06  Matched requests table   ──►   field: F-MATCHES        (what this element shows)
                               ◄──   used on: page-approvals.html#EL-06

With that in place:

  • The page's Data and fields block is rendered from the dictionary instead of being retyped, so the two cannot disagree.
  • The checker can fail a page whose element shows a field that is not in the catalogue, or whose field sits below the rung its gate requires.
  • "Which pages are affected if this source is late?" and "which fields on this page are still at L0?" each become one query.
  • A figure that appears on nine screens keeps one definition, permanently.

7. What to do first

  1. Give every field an identifier, and every element on a mockument page the identifier of the field it shows.
  2. Bind periodicity, visibility and provenance to their vocabularies.
  3. Split the notes column into question · owner · needed by, and push the open ones to named people.
  4. Take the hero fields to L2 now. Leave everything else at L0.
  5. Then work the ladder page by page, as pages pass business review.

The test of whether this is working: at any moment you can say, for any page, what rung every field on it has reached and who owes the next answer. If the dictionary can answer that, the question of when to do field-level work never has to be argued again.
